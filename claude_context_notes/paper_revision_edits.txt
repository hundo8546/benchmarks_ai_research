SUGGESTED TITLE: "Generalized K-Stage Formulation and Three Operating Regimes"

CONTENT TO ADD (ready to paste, adjust equation labels/numbers to fit
surrounding numbering):

---
The three-detector pipeline evaluated in this paper is an instance of a more
general $K$-stage cascade. Let a sequence of detectors $f_1, \ldots, f_K$
have non-decreasing cost $C_1 \le C_2 \le \cdots \le C_K$ and accuracies
$A_1, \ldots, A_K$ on a fixed evaluation distribution. Define the accuracy
gap between adjacent stages as $\Delta_{i,i+1} = A_{i+1} - A_i$. A cascade
only benefits from escalating stage $i$ to stage $i{+}1$ on a subset of
inputs if $\Delta_{i,i+1}$ restricted to that subset is positive; the
unconditional, dataset-wide comparison of $A_i$ and $A_{i+1}$ is not
sufficient, since escalation is never applied uniformly.

This paper instantiates $K=3$ with $f_1 = f_{\text{art}}$, $f_2 = f_{\text{sem}}$,
$f_3 = f_{\text{vlm}}$, restricting escalation to the disagreement set
$D = \{x : \hat y_{\text{art}}(x) \ne \hat y_{\text{sem}}(x)\}$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:disagree}). Cascade accuracy decomposes as
\[
A_{\text{cascade}} = (1-p_D)\, A_{\text{agree}} + p_D\, A_{3\mid D},
\]
where $p_D = P(x \in D)$, $A_{\text{agree}}$ is accuracy on the agreement set
(identical for $f_1$ and $f_2$ by construction, Section III.C), and
$A_{3\mid D}$ is verifier accuracy restricted to $D$. Because the agreement
term is shared with the semantic-only baseline, the sign of cascade gain over
semantic-only depends only on the disagreement subset:
\[
\text{Gain} = A_{\text{cascade}} - A_2 \;\approx\; p_D \left( A_{3\mid D} - A_{2\mid D} \right),
\]
where $A_{2\mid D}$ is the semantic detector's own accuracy restricted to $D$
(typically far below its dataset-wide accuracy, since $D$ is precisely where
$f_2$ is contested). This formalizes three regimes:

Case 1 (both cheap stages strong, $A_1 \approx A_2 \approx A_3$): $p_D \to 0$
so Gain $\to 0$ regardless of $A_{3\mid D}$; the verifier's quality is nearly
irrelevant because it is invoked on almost nothing.

Case 2 (asymmetric cheap stages, $A_1 \ll A_2$, $A_2 \gtrsim A_3$): $D$ is
dominated by cases where the artifact detector is wrong and the semantic
detector is right. If $A_{3\mid D} < A_{2\mid D}$, Gain $< 0$: the cascade
trades away correct semantic predictions for the verifier's mixed accuracy
on $D$. Any positive delta over the artifact-only baseline in this regime
reflects the semantic detector's quality gap over the artifact detector, not
the verifier or the routing mechanism.

Case 3 (both cheap stages degraded, $A_1 \ll A_3$, $A_2 \ll A_3$): $D$ is
common because neither cheap detector is individually reliable, and if the
verifier retains high accuracy on $D$, Gain is reliably positive and large,
attributable specifically to escalation.

Breakdown condition: disagreement is informative only when (a) $f_1, f_2$
errors are weakly correlated, so $p_D$ stays large enough to route a
meaningful fraction of hard cases, and (b) $A_{2\mid D} < A_{3\mid D}$ on the
routed subset specifically. If $f_1, f_2$ share a correlated blind spot, $D$
collapses toward the small residual where they happen to differ, $p_D \to 0$,
and cascade accuracy reduces to $A_{\text{agree}}$ even though both detectors
may be jointly wrong on the uncaught correlated-error inputs — those failures
are invisible to the routing rule by construction.
---
